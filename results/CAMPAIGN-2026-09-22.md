## R1 — why, and what it fixes

Paper 2's Table 3 (paired statistics) and all of Table 4 (SIFT) were
measured on the **one-sided** candidate kernel, before the symmetric scan.
Every kernel revision produces a byte-identical graph, so the quality
columns are unaffected and the published times are conservative — but the
paper has to say so in §11. R1 removes the caveat.

Armset `dense2` in `bench/d0_phase1.py` (the old `dense` armset is kept
verbatim for reproducibility). GloVe pairs the spine-on arm (the
connectivity guarantee, expected ~95.5 s) *and* the spine-off arm
(expected ~84 s) because the paper reports both. SIFT adds the L700 and
L1400 pool arms, which the paper currently carries as single builds.

Gate: block 0's dense arm must reproduce ~95.5 s with completeness 0.613,

## R3 — Deep-100M, the experiment the scaling claim needs

Paper 2 states the scaling question as: does the data require a fixed
*fraction* f (quadratic, a 10^6 design) or a fixed *absolute* pool P = fn
(linear)? GloVe reached parity at P = 1.06e5 and Deep-10M at P = 1.04e5 —
the same absolute pool an order of magnitude apart in n, but the two
corpora also differ in intrinsic dimension, so two points cannot separate
the effects. Deep-100M at P = 1e5 decides it.

Parameters from finding 24: L = 24,000, m = 5, C = 200, cap 64, alpha = 1,
Vamana ending, spine on. Candidates ~1e13 pairs; at the measured Deep rate
(1.01 G unordered pairs/s) that is 2.7–3 h, but at 10^8 the heaps are
160 GB and the offers leave cache, so budget **3–5 h**. Prune ~15 min,
spine ~6 min. Baseline: Vamana R64/L128 alpha1.2 two-pass, 2–3 h (699 s at
10^7). GRAFT's 10^8 arm is already measured (16,700 s) — no re-run.

**R2 is the blocker and does not need d0.** The membership stage as written
costs n*L = 2.4e12 distances (~40 min) at L = 24,000; a two-level hierarchy
(240 top leaders x 100 sub-leaders) makes it n*340 = 3.4e10 (~35 s). The
implementation must keep Proposition 1: hash identical at 1 vs 64 threads,
and a Deep-10M regression showing the hierarchy does not move the graph
against the flat path.

## R4 — the largest engineering headroom in paper 2

fg's 4x4 tile has no blocking over the dimension: 0.0786 G pairs/s at
d = 960 against OpenBLAS's 0.606 (W = 7.7), versus W = 2.3 at d = 100,
because eight rows of 960 floats is 30 KB and overflows L1. Splitting d
into ~1 KB chunks with accumulator carry should take GIST's candidate stage
from 336 s toward 90 s and the build to ~0.3x Vamana. The gate is bitwise
identity of the GloVe and GIST graphs. It will **not** help Deep-100M
(d = 96 is already near the tile's best), so it does not block R3.

## R2 result (2026-09-22, laptop): the hierarchy works, and should not be used

`--dense-top T` / `--dense-probe p` implemented and gated: bitwise
deterministic at 1 vs 10 threads, and `T=0` leaves the flat path untouched.
GloVe L=240, 10 threads, `results/hier/glove_hier.log`:

| T | leader dist | candidates | max leaf | compl. | d@0.94 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 (flat) | 764.0 M | **83.6 s** | 61,122 | **0.613** | 8,095 |
| 16 | 582.4 M | 101.5 s (+21%) | 68,907 | 0.602 | 8,079 |
| 32 | 557.9 M | 112.0 s (+34%) | 68,174 | 0.590 | 8,009 |

The membership scan does get 2.8x cheaper. But the approximate assignment
concentrates points into fewer distinct leaders, **max leaf grows 61k -> 69k,
total pairs rise 4% and the candidate stage rises 21-34%** — the candidate
stage is the whole build, so the trade is badly negative. Completeness also
falls 1.8-3.8% relative.

**The premise was wrong, and the arithmetic says so.** MEMO/finding-24
budgeted the flat membership at 10^8 as 2.4e12 distances = ~40 min and
treated that as a blocker. Measured at Deep-10M it is **20.5 s of a 734 s
build, 2.8%**; scaling by n*L to 10^8 with L=24,000 gives ~34 min of a
~3-4 h build, ~15%. The hierarchy would save ~34 min and cost 20-34% of a
1.5-2 h candidate stage, i.e. 20-40 min, plus quality. Net zero at best.

**Decision: R3 runs with the FLAT (exact) membership.** `--dense-top` stays
in the tree as a measured fallback with its cost recorded here, off by
default. **R3 is therefore unblocked now — the day of implementation the
schedule allowed for is not needed.**

Revised R3 estimate from the Deep-10M measurement rather than the
projection: pairs at P=1e5 are 5e12 unordered = 8.14x Deep-10M's 614 G, so
the candidate stage is ~1.4 h at the measured 1.01 G/s; membership ~34 min;
prune ~15 min; spine ~6 min. **Budget 3-4 h** (the 160 GB of heaps will
push the offer path out of cache, which the Deep-10M rate does not capture).

## 1. The absolute-pool hypothesis holds for the candidate stage

Candidate pairs grew **11.1x for 10x the points** (614 G -> 6,788 G; the
excess over 10x is leaf variance). At a fixed absolute pool the candidate
stage is linear in n, as the paper conjectured from two points. It is now
measured at three.

The wall time grew more (606.8 s -> 11,381 s, 18.8x) because the **rate fell
1.70x**, 1.012 -> 0.596 G unordered pairs/s: the heaps are n*C*8 = 160 GB
and the offers leave cache. That is a constant-factor locality problem, not
a complexity change.

## 2. Quality: the dense graph now BEATS Vamana, and the margin grows with n

| corpus | P | build vs Vamana | d@0.95 | d@0.99 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GloVe 10^6 | 1.06e5 | **0.67x** | parity | parity |
| Deep 10^7 | 1.04e5 | 1.05x | +1.4% | -6.7% |
| Deep 10^8 | 1.0e5 | 1.55x | **-6.7%** | **-16.3%** |

Two opposing trends, both explicable. The dense graph's candidate quality is
n-independent at a fixed absolute pool, while the search builder's per-point
cost grows — Vamana's build work grew **14.9x for 10x the points** (278 G ->
4,151 G), i.e. its per-point cost rose 49%. So quality moves our way with
scale. Build time moves the other way, for the two reasons in §1 and §3.

## 3. The real asymptotic barrier is the MEMBERSHIP stage, not the candidates

At a fixed absolute pool P, L = m^2 n / P, so the membership scan costs
n*L = **m^2 n^2 / P — quadratic in n**:

| n | membership dist | share of build |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10^7 | 2.4e10 | 20.5 s, 2.8% |
| 10^8 | 2.4e12 | 1,942 s, **13%** |
| 10^9 (projected) | 2.4e14 | ~55 h, **~64%** |

**This partially reverses the R2 verdict.** The two-level hierarchy was
refuted on GloVe because it cost 20-34% of the candidate stage in leaf
imbalance while saving a membership stage worth 2.8%. At 10^8 the membership
is 13% and the trade is roughly even; **beyond 10^8 the hierarchy (or some
sublinear router over the leaders) stops being optional.** The cheaper exact
fix — blocking the membership scan, which is perfectly GEMM-shaped at
n x L — buys ~3.2x (1.38 G/s measured against d0's 4.45 G/s SGEMM) but does
not touch the exponent.

So the paper's scale section should say: linear in the candidate stage,
quadratic in the membership stage, measured to 10^8, with the crossover
where the second overtakes the first falling between 10^8 and 10^9.

# R4 RESULT — adaptive tile size, and SIFT re-measured (2026-09-23)

## The fix

Per-leaf tile size: largest power of two in [32,128] leaving >= ~100 blocks.
Fitted on a 64-thread sweep (`results/tb_sweep_d0.log`) plus the laptop
sweep; within 11% of the measured optimum in all six (leaf, threads) cases.
Every leaf >= 20k stays at TB=128, so GloVe, SIFT L240 and Deep-10M/100M are
unchanged -- R1's GloVe result and R3's 4:13:06 Deep-100M build stand.

TB never changes the graph (identical edges, max degree and distance counts
at every TB): the heap rule is commutative, so tile size is a pure
scheduling parameter. A search builder has no such knob -- changing its
schedule changes its graph.

**The SIFT conclusion changes.** The paper says the dense frontier merely
*coincides* with the frozen search builder's and does not beat Vamana. With
the shipped kernel a 3% pool is at **Vamana's build time and Vamana's
quality** (+3.9% at 0.98, -0.8% at 0.99), and it beats \graft-T4 on both
axes at equal cost. SIFT is a draw with the tuned incremental baseline, not
a loss.

The L1400 arm no longer beats L700 (52.9 vs 51.5 s): once the candidate
stage is ~40 s, shrinking the pool stops paying because the *leaders* stage
grows (1.94 -> 3.81 s). The dense frontier on SIFT bottoms out at ~51 s.

**This is the best build ratio in the paper**, better than GloVe's 0.67x, on

work is fixed by the pool. Quality is at or better than Vamana through recall
0.97 and falls behind at 0.98, where the dense graph's lower degree (39.4 vs
60.4) starts to bite.

30x larger than the 200k slice originally planned.
